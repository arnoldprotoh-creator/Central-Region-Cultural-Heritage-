Places that hold memory

Central District heritage

From the Tswapong Hills to Serowe, the region’s landscapes, historic places and community stories connect people to the past and to one another.

Historic settlement · Phalatswe

Old Palapye

Old Palapye, also called Phalatswe, lies at the foot of the Tswapong Hills near Malaka village. It became an important Bangwato settlement in the late nineteenth century. Its surviving ruins, including the remains of a church, offer a visible connection to the history of the area and to the leadership of Kgosi Khama III.

Today, the site helps visitors explore how settlement, religion, leadership and changing political circumstances shaped the region. Visitors should respect the ruins and follow local heritage guidance.

Photograph: Wikimedia Commons, “Old Palapye ruins” (CC BY-SA 4.0).

Landscape · Water & livelihoods

Lecheng Pan

Lecheng Pan is a local landscape feature associated with the Lecheng area of the Central District. Pans and seasonal wetlands can support birds, livestock, plant life and local livelihoods, while also reflecting the region’s changing rainfall and water conditions.

For a school project, local knowledge is especially important here. Add a community-verified photograph and a short interview with a resident or local guide to document the pan’s name, seasonal changes, uses and stories accurately. The image shown here is a broad Tswapong–Lotsane landscape view, not a verified close-up of Lecheng Pan.

Natural & spiritual heritage

Moremi Gorge

Moremi Gorge lies deep in the Tswapong Hills and is known for rocky cliffs, permanent waterfalls and pools. It is also a place of spiritual importance to the Tswapong community and is recognised as a national monument. The gorge’s natural features and cultural meanings belong together: the landscape is not only scenery, but also part of community memory and identity.

Visitors should seek local guidance and permission where required, stay on safe routes, and respect sacred places and the environment.

Photograph: Hanani Khupe via Wikimedia Commons (CC BY-SA 4.0).

Community conservation · Serowe

Khama Rhino Sanctuary

Located near Serowe, Khama Rhino Sanctuary was established in 1992 as a community-based wildlife project. It provides habitat for endangered rhinos and other wildlife while supporting conservation awareness and local tourism.

The sanctuary connects natural heritage with community action: protecting wildlife also protects ecological knowledge and creates opportunities for education and responsible tourism.
